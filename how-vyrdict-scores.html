How VYRDICT scores the hype.

Virality and value are not the same thing. VYRDICT keeps them separate so a product can be everywhere on your feed without automatically being called worth your money.

The goal: separate attention from actual value
01

Viral Score

A 0–100 measure of how strongly a product is showing up in the current conversation. Higher means more visible, faster-moving, or more culturally present right now.

We look across signals such as social attention, search interest, creator activity and momentum. A high Viral Score tells you how loud the hype is — not whether the product is good.

Worth Score

A separate 0–100 assessment of whether the product appears to deliver enough real-world value to justify the hype and price.

It synthesizes consumer experience, ratings and sentiment, repeat-use or repurchase signals, price/value context, category expectations and hype-risk factors such as unusually heavy sponsorship or controversy.

The Skip List.

The Skip List is intentionally stricter than a normal verdict. It highlights products with extremely high attention but a comparatively weak Worth Score.

Products meeting this threshold can enter The Skip List, with higher virality and weaker worth prioritized first. It is a VYRDICT editorial/data assessment — not a claim that a product is unsafe or universally bad.

Freshness

Scores are not meant to sit still.

Trend data changes. Prices change. Retailer pages disappear. Products improve, reformulate, or fall out of the conversation. VYRDICT therefore tracks verification and review timing behind the scenes.

Product freshness

Each active product has a last-verified timestamp and a next-review date so old viral data can be revisited rather than treated as permanent.

Images & retailer freshness

Verified product imagery and shopping routes are tracked separately. When an exact image or retailer page cannot be confirmed, VYRDICT would rather leave the field pending than invent a match.

Important context

VYRDICT scores are structured editorial/data signals, not laboratory testing, medical advice, financial advice, or a guarantee that every person will have the same experience. Categories such as skincare, wellness and fitness can be especially individual. The scores are designed to make hype easier to evaluate — not to replace professional guidance where it matters.
