Case Study 01Culture × Product

McCafé

When a coffee tray turned into a fashion accessory, the internet got a perfect example of why everyday rituals can become headline-worthy product stories.

The moment

The McCafé tray-bag concept transformed an ordinary coffee run into a visual talking point—playful, recognizable, and instantly shareable.

What we’d do

Build a creator-led moment around “the accessory your morning routine didn’t know it needed,” pairing editorial storytelling, UGC hooks, and launch-day conversation starters.

What it shows

VYRDICT can identify when a familiar object is about to become a cultural conversation, then translate that into a campaign angle with internet appeal.

Turn the ordinary into something people want to post.
What looks simple offline can become magnetic online when the idea is visual, timely, and culturally fluent.

Case Study 02Community × Creators

Rothy’s

A strong brand doesn’t always need a giant celebrity moment—sometimes it needs the right small-room energy with the right community.

The moment

Rothy’s community-style activation showed the value of gathering tastemakers in a way that feels intimate, trusted, and naturally shareable.

What we’d do

Position the event around a specific audience behavior or identity, create a cleaner pre-event story hook, and turn the guest mix into part of the content strategy.

What it shows

VYRDICT understands how to turn community energy into a brand story—not just an event recap, but a reason for the internet to care.

Small communities can create big internet signals.
Relevance doesn’t always come from scale. It often comes from credibility, curation, and the right host audience fit.

Case Study 03Beauty × Product Culture

rhode

Some products work harder online because the product itself is already content—and that’s exactly where strategic storytelling gets interesting.

The moment

rhode’s product design makes content creation intuitive. The item looks good on camera, fits existing consumer behavior, and naturally invites showing, sharing, and styling.

What we’d do

Build a content framework around “product as ritual, product as image, product as identity,” so creator content and owned content feel visually seamless.

What it shows

VYRDICT can spot why a product becomes socially magnetic—and turn that into a scalable creative idea brands can own.

If the product is already content, the strategy should amplify that.
The best launches don’t only perform because people like them—they perform because they fit how people already document their lives.
