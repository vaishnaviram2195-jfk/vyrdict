The good taste club

What people with exceptional taste give.

A case for the beautifully ordinary, the peculiar and the deeply personal.

VYRDICT Editorial · November 26, 2026

Good taste in gifting is not about having an extravagant budget. It is about noticing the detail that turns an otherwise ordinary object into something memorable. Our editorial rule is simple: give someone a little more of the life they already love.

Find the exceptional version of an everyday thing.

A sculptural olive-oil pourer for the friend who cooks. A beautiful comb for the person who has a nightly hair ritual. A tiny ceramic vessel for the collector of things that are entirely impractical and entirely delightful. The object need not be rare; its design should make an everyday moment feel special.

Shop for their particularities.

Think beyond broad categories such as fashion lover or foodie. Notice the colour they always wear, the coffee they order, the designer whose work they admire, the book they mention repeatedly. Specificity makes a gift feel chosen rather than purchased on autopilot.

Give permission to indulge.

Consider a small workshop with a favourite local maker, an appointment with a perfumer, an exquisite pantry ingredient or a piece of art from an emerging artist. Match the indulgence to their actual interests and circumstances, not to an imagined version of them.

Good taste is attention, not a price tag.

These are VYRDICT’s editorial suggestions, not interviews with named tastemakers. For another perspective, fashion editors' published gifting selections offer useful inspiration for the kinds of objects that catch an experienced eye.
