VYRDICT OPPORTUNITY SCORE

Is this opportunity
worth pursuing?

We look at five things: attention, brand fit, lasting relevance, creator stories and interest in buying. Each gets up to 20 points. Together, they give us a score out of 100 and a practical next step.

The five factorsWhat your score meansSee an example
THE PROCESS

Five factors. Equal weight.

  1. 01

    Define the opportunity.

    Name the brand, the audience and the goal. An idea can be a strong fit for one brand and a weak fit for another.

  2. 02

    Rate the evidence.

    Give each factor 0–20 points using the guide below. Explain the reason, link the source and call out anything still unknown.

  3. 03

    Add up. Choose a next step.

    All five factors count equally. The total points us toward a focused test, a change to the idea, more research or a pass.

The exact number is an editorial judgement. It helps us compare opportunities; the strength of the evidence matters as much as the total.

THE FIVE QUESTIONS

What earns the points?

Every factor uses the same four ranges. Open a question to see what we look for and how we rate it.

0–5 · UnprovenLittle support or too little information.
6–10 · EarlySome clues, with limited support.
11–15 · PromisingUseful signals, with gaps to check.
16–20 · StrongClear, relevant support.
Buzz · up to 20 points Is attention growing and spreading?

Evidence we look for

Look for changes in relevant searches, conversation, coverage and sharing. Compare a defined time period and separate paid attention from organic attention.

How we rate it

0–5
Little useful evidence of attention, or reach is unknown.
6–10
A launch, coverage or conversation offers early clues.
11–15
Attention is spreading among relevant people, with some corroboration.
16–20
Sustained growth appears across relevant channels or communities.
Brand fit · up to 20 points Does it make sense for this brand and its audience?

Evidence we look for

Check the product, audience, brand values and business goal. Use audience research, customer feedback and the brand brief where available.

How we rate it

0–5
The connection is unclear or conflicts with the brand.
6–10
There is a loose connection, but the audience or purpose is unclear.
11–15
It fits the brand and a clear audience or goal, with gaps to check.
16–20
There is a strong, credible link to the product, audience and goal.
Staying power · up to 20 points Can it last beyond the first spike?

Evidence we look for

Look for repeat behaviour, a continuing customer need and room for more than one story. Check whether interest holds after the launch.

How we rate it

0–5
It relies on a single novelty moment, with little reason to return.
6–10
A repeat use or story is possible but lightly supported.
11–15
A continuing need and repeatable story are plausible.
16–20
Repeated behaviour or activity supports a lasting opportunity.
Creator potential · up to 20 points Can the right creators tell a story people care about?

Evidence we look for

Look at creator credibility, audience fit, natural product use and possible stories. More followers alone do not earn more points.

How we rate it

0–5
A natural creator story is hard to identify.
6–10
There is a possible hook, but creator fit is unclear.
11–15
There are clear stories and plausible creator partners.
16–20
Credible creators can make varied, natural stories around the idea.
Buying intent · up to 20 points Are people showing interest in buying?

Evidence we look for

Look for product questions, qualified store visits, waitlist activity, add-to-cart activity or purchases. Record what each signal actually measures.

How we rate it

0–5
Buying interest is unverified; a product listing alone is not enough.
6–10
Soft interest appears, such as saves or general enquiries.
11–15
Specific product questions or shopping actions show interest.
16–20
Consistent commercial signals support demand, with a clear measurement period.

Within a range, broader and more consistent support earns more points. Record the reason for the number so someone else can follow the judgement.

What if evidence is missing? Say so. Little information belongs in the unproven range and lowers our confidence. A low rating can mean “we need to find out,” rather than “this cannot work.”
FROM SCORE TO ACTION

What does the total mean?

80–100GO

Worth a focused test.

60–79ADAPT

Improve the weak points, then test.

40–59WATCH

Wait for stronger evidence.

0–39PASS

Put effort elsewhere for now.

GO means the opportunity is worth a focused test. Before committing, agree the budget, practical constraints and what success would look like. Even a strong total can hide a weak factor that needs attention.

ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE86/100GO · Worth a focused test

Next step: run a small launch with clear measures for attention, action and sales.

  • Buzz 14/20

    Assume relevant conversation is growing, but is still concentrated in a few communities.

  • Brand fit 19/20

    Assume audience research shows a strong connection to the product and the brand’s goal.

  • Staying power 17/20

    Assume people return to the behaviour over time and there is room for follow-up stories.

  • Creator potential 19/20

    Assume credible creators have several natural ways to use and talk about the product.

  • Buying intent 17/20

    Assume a test shows qualified shopping activity and purchases, beyond likes and views.

14 + 19 + 17 + 19 + 17 = 86/100

MAKE IT CHECKABLE

Keep the reasons beside the score.

  • Record the opportunity, brand, audience, goal and assessment date.
  • Give each factor a reason and a source. Separate observed facts from our interpretation.
  • Include the measurement period and explain whether signals came from organic attention, paid activity or a brand’s own channels.
  • State the gaps and our confidence. Update the score when better evidence arrives.
